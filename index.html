open entrainer

Two tones, one in each ear, a few hertz apart. The brain hears the difference as a slow beat and tends to follow it.

How it works

A binaural beat is not in the sound. The left ear gets one frequency, the right ear another, and the brainstem, comparing the two, constructs a pulse at their difference. With 100 Hz on the left and 104 Hz on the right, that pulse is 4 Hz, which is in the theta range. Over minutes, brainwave activity tends to drift toward the beat. This only works with headphones; through speakers the two tones mix in the air and the effect is gone.

A session has three phases. The ramp in takes the beat from a waking frequency down to the target. The hold keeps it there. The ramp out brings it back up so you do not surface too fast. The transitions follow a logarithmic curve with soft ends, because the body resists sudden changes of state.

Pink noise under the tones covers the room and makes the pure sines easier to sit with. Everything is generated in this page while it plays. Nothing is downloaded, nothing is sent anywhere, and the settings stay in your browser.

Using it

  • Put on stereo headphones and keep the volume low.
  • Pick a target. Around 4 to 6 Hz for sleep onset or a long lying meditation, 8 to 10 Hz for resting with eyes closed, 14 to 20 Hz for focused work.
  • Set the three durations, or set an end time and let the hold stretch to meet it. For a session that should end when the alarm goes, this is the easier way.
  • Start, close your eyes, and let it run. The session stops itself at the end.

Before you use it

  • This is a self-help tool, not a medical device. It does not treat anything.
  • Do not use it while driving or operating anything that needs your attention.
  • With epilepsy, a pacemaker, or a psychiatric condition, ask a clinician first.
  • Stop if you feel unwell. Use it at your own responsibility.
